Milestone 1 asks one question: on the same budget of evaluations, does an LLM steering the exploration do better than plain optimisers with no LLM? The CORDIC design space is small enough (<V of="designs" fmt="int" /> designs) to score every design, so every run is graded against the exact answer. Each method ran three seeds on each of four specs, 400 evaluations per run at most. The agents used three real models through OpenRouter, one of them (Qwen3.8-27B) both with its default reasoning and with reasoning off: <V of="runs" fmt="int" /> recorded runs, <V of="calls.total" fmt="int" /> LLM calls, <V of="total_usd" fmt="usd" /> of provider-reported usage.

## Where the agent lost

Plainly, first. **NSGA-II maps the trade-off curve better than any agent on two of the three feasible specs.** On `dds_250msps` it covers <V of="dds_250msps.nsga2_hv" fmt="f3" /> of the true front's hypervolume, against <V of="dds_250msps.min_agent_hv" fmt="f3" />–<V of="dds_250msps.best_agent_hv" fmt="f3" /> for the agents; on `low_area_control`, <V of="low_area_control.nsga2_hv" fmt="f3" /> against <V of="low_area_control.min_agent_hv" fmt="f3" />–<V of="low_area_control.best_agent_hv" fmt="f3" />. The agents dive at the corner of the space the spec cares about, the front stops growing there, and the 1% hypervolume-gain rule ends the run with budget unspent. Random search beats every agent on hypervolume on those two specs as well.

And on selection, one model-and-spec pair lost too: Qwen3.8-27B with reasoning off, on `low_area_control`, picked designs <V of="low_area_control.qwen_off_regret" fmt="spct" /> worse than the optimum on average, against NSGA-II's <V of="low_area_control.nsga2_regret" fmt="spct" />. The other <V of="regret.agent_beats_nsga2" fmt="int" /> of the <V of="regret.agent_rows" fmt="int" /> pairs beat NSGA-II's mean regret.

## Where it won

**The agent is the better selector.** On every feasible spec, the best agent picked a design closer to the true optimum than either baseline: <V of="dds_250msps.best_agent_regret" fmt="spct" /> against NSGA-II's <V of="dds_250msps.nsga2_regret" fmt="spct" /> on `dds_250msps`, <V of="high_precision.best_agent_regret" fmt="spct" /> against <V of="high_precision.nsga2_regret" fmt="spct" /> on `high_precision`, <V of="low_area_control.best_agent_regret" fmt="spct" /> against <V of="low_area_control.nsga2_regret" fmt="spct" /> on `low_area_control`. On `high_precision`, whose true front is a single point, the agents also win on hypervolume (Sonnet 5.5 covers <V of="high_precision.sonnet_hv" fmt="f3" />; evaluations to 95%: <V of="high_precision.sonnet_e95" fmt="str" />, against NSGA-II's <V of="high_precision.nsga2_e95" fmt="str" />). **And on the infeasible spec, every model on every seed declared it infeasible itself** (<V of="infeasible.llm_declared" fmt="int" /> of <V of="infeasible.llm_runs" fmt="int" /> runs), naming throughput as the binding constraint, usually after 100–200 evaluations rather than the whole budget. The baselines can only report that they found nothing.

That split is the division of labour the design intends. The agent finds the critical 3% cheaply: the right design for this spec, or the proof that none exists. Mapping the whole front exhaustively stays the job of a classic optimiser, which the agent drives.
