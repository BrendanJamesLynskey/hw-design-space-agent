The eval asks one question: on the same budget of evaluations, does an LLM steering the exploration do better than plain optimisers with no LLM? The CORDIC design space is small enough (<V of="designs" fmt="int" /> designs) to score every design, so every run is graded against the exact answer, always with the same (M1) cost model so the milestones are comparable.

Milestone 1 found a split: the agent picked better designs than NSGA-II and random search, but mapped the trade-off curve far worse, because it dived at the spec's corner and stopped early. Milestone 2 set out to close that gap without losing the selection lead: a `map_front` decision, a 40% front-mapping reserve that code spends before a run stops, and five seeds instead of three. It ran three models through OpenRouter, Sonnet 5.5, DeepSeek V4.1 Flash and Qwen3.8-27B with reasoning off: <V of="m2.runs" fmt="int" /> runs, <V of="m2.calls.total" fmt="int" /> LLM calls, <V of="m2.total_usd" fmt="usd" /> of provider-reported usage. Every table below is mean ± population standard deviation over the seeds.

Milestone 3 then put the design inside its system and tried a free-form agent on top of the structured one. Its results come first; milestone 2's and milestone 1's follow, as they were.

## Milestone 3 at a glance

- **A free-form campaign agent gained nothing over the structured graph, at a higher cost.** On the same specs, seeds and budget, its hypervolume and regret, averaged over the specs, were slightly worse for every model, it cost <V of="m3.ab.cost_ratio_min" fmt="f2" />–<V of="m3.ab.cost_ratio_max" fmt="f2" />× as much per run, Qwen looped in <V of="m3.ab.qwen.failed" fmt="int" /> of its <V of="m3.ab.qwen.runs" fmt="int" /> campaigns, and in <V of="m3.ab.single" fmt="int" /> of <V of="m3.ab.campaigns" fmt="int" /> campaigns its plan came down to one call to the structured graph. It stays experimental, off by default.
- **Cross-run memory showed no benefit** in the one model and three seeds it was tested on.
- **System-level simulation changes the winner where the system is the hard part.** On `multiaxis_control` only the simulation finds the right design (m=4); on `bursty_offload` a back-of-envelope peak-rate floor finds the same winner, and on `dds_sfdr` nothing changes, a negative result the repository keeps.
- **The structured graph keeps its M2 pattern on the new specs:** hypervolume at or above NSGA-II's (one exception, by 0.002) and much lower regret.

## System specs: what the simulation changes

Three new specs constrain the system, not just the unit: a batch deadline, a p99 latency under bursts, a DDS that must never stall with a spurious-free dynamic range of at least 90 dBc. Each has an exhaustive ground truth, simulated for every one of the <V of="designs" fmt="int" /> designs, and three more views of it: what the L1 bound sees, the spec read in milestone-2 terms (a throughput floor at the system's _average_ rate), and, recomputed for this site, a floor at the _peak_ rate the system needs.

<SystemSpecsTable />

- **`multiaxis_control`: the simulation is needed.** The average-rate view picks m=7, the L1 bound and a peak-rate floor both pick m=5, and m=5 misses the simulated 0.44 µs deadline by <V of="m3.multi.miss" fmt="pct" />, because a tick lands between clock edges and up to a cycle passes before the first accept. Only the simulation gets to m=4 (<V of="m3.multi.feasible" fmt="int" /> designs pass it, <V of="m3.multi.feasible_bound" fmt="int" /> pass the bound). The [replay on How it works](/how-it-works) shows it request by request.
- **`bursty_offload`: average-rate sizing is wrong, but simulation is not needed.** In milestone-2 terms the winner is the small FSM design that wins `low_area_control` (<V of="m3.bursty.msps_lf" fmt="int" /> LUT+FF), but it takes one request every <V of="m3.bursty.msps_ii" fmt="int" /> cycles, so the eighth request of a burst waits for the other seven: its simulated p99 is <V of="m3.bursty.msps_p99" fmt="f2" /> µs, <V of="m3.bursty.msps_ratio" fmt="x" /> even its bound. The winner is a `pipelined_m` at <V of="m3.bursty.area_ratio" fmt="x" /> the area. But sizing for the burst (8 requests in 0.4 µs, <V of="m3.bursty.peak_floor" fmt="int" /> MSPS) gives the same winner and the same <V of="m3.bursty.feasible" fmt="int" /> designs.
- **`dds_sfdr`: a negative result.** For this design space the DDS's system constraints reduce to the throughput-only view: the throughput-only winner already has <V of="m3.dds.sfdr" fmt="f1" /> dBc spurious-free dynamic range, and "no stall" is "throughput ≥ the sample rate". The repository keeps it as evidence and does not evaluate agents on it.

## Where it got worse

Plainly, first.

- **Qwen on `high_precision`** lost to NSGA-II on selection: <V of="glance.qwen_hp_regret_m2" fmt="spct" /> mean regret (± <V of="glance.qwen_hp_regret_m2_std" fmt="pct" />) against NSGA-II's <V of="m2.high_precision.nsga2_regret" fmt="spct" />, up from <V of="glance.qwen_hp_regret_m1" fmt="spct" /> in M1. - **DeepSeek's selection got worse:** <V of="glance.ds_dds_regret_m1" fmt="spct" /> → <V of="glance.ds_dds_regret_m2" fmt="spct" /> on `dds_250msps` and <V of="glance.ds_low_regret_m1" fmt="spct" /> → <V of="glance.ds_low_regret_m2" fmt="spct" /> on `low_area_control`.
