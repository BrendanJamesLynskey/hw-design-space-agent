The eval asks one question: on the same budget of evaluations, does an LLM steering the exploration do better than plain optimisers with no LLM? The CORDIC design space is small enough (<V of="designs" fmt="int" /> designs) to score every design, so every run is graded against the exact answer, always with the same (M1) cost model so the milestones are comparable.

Milestone 1 found a split: the agent picked better designs than NSGA-II and random search, but mapped the trade-off curve far worse, because it dived at the spec's corner and stopped early. Milestone 2 set out to close that gap without losing the selection lead: a `map_front` decision, a 40% front-mapping reserve that code spends before a run stops, and five seeds instead of three. It ran three models through OpenRouter, Sonnet 5.5, DeepSeek V4.1 Flash and Qwen3.8-27B with reasoning off: <V of="m2.runs" fmt="int" /> runs, <V of="m2.calls.total" fmt="int" /> LLM calls, <V of="m2.total_usd" fmt="usd" /> of provider-reported usage. Every table below is mean ± population standard deviation over the seeds.

## Where it got worse

Plainly, first.

- **Qwen on `high_precision`** lost to NSGA-II on selection: <V of="glance.qwen_hp_regret_m2" fmt="spct" /> mean regret (± <V of="glance.qwen_hp_regret_m2_std" fmt="pct" />) against NSGA-II's <V of="m2.high_precision.nsga2_regret" fmt="spct" />, up from <V of="glance.qwen_hp_regret_m1" fmt="spct" /> in M1. One seed accounts for it: its first box for `pipelined_m` was too narrow to reach the accuracy target, the front became a single `pipelined` design, and front-mapping only revisits the families on the front, so the winner's family never came back. That seed selected a design <V of="glance.qwen_hp_worst_seed" fmt="spct" /> from the optimum; the median of its five seeds is <V of="glance.qwen_hp_median" fmt="spct" />. The same seed pulls its `high_precision` hypervolume below NSGA-II's (the one cell under 1.0 above). The repository proposes a fix for milestone 3 and did not apply it after seeing the eval.
- **DeepSeek's selection got worse:** <V of="glance.ds_dds_regret_m1" fmt="spct" /> → <V of="glance.ds_dds_regret_m2" fmt="spct" /> on `dds_250msps` and <V of="glance.ds_low_regret_m1" fmt="spct" /> → <V of="glance.ds_low_regret_m2" fmt="spct" /> on `low_area_control`. It chose `map_front` in <V of="glance.mf_deepseek" fmt="int" /> of its <V of="glance.mf_runs" fmt="int" /> feasible-spec runs (Qwen <V of="glance.mf_qwen" fmt="int" />, Sonnet <V of="glance.mf_sonnet" fmt="int" />), spending its rounds on coverage rather than on the corner. It still beats NSGA-II on both.
- **The agent now always uses the whole budget** on feasible specs, 400 evaluations like the baselines, by design: the reserve spends what is left on mapping. M1's "fewer evaluations" came with its narrow fronts.
- Five seeds is still a small sample: several cells overlap within one standard deviation (DeepSeek on `low_area_control`, for one).
